\begin{abstract}
An existing dexterous grasp expert encodes more than a terminal hand pose:
grasp types and contact structure describe different ways to hold an object.
We investigate whether this knowledge can be transferred through synthetic
demonstrations to a visual policy that grasps a requested object part, without
collecting new real-robot grasp demonstrations for the target platform.
The proposed DexPrompt pipeline uses Dexonomy as a training-time synthesis
teacher, converts candidates into scene-feasible approach--close--lift
demonstrations, and provides an implemented DexGraspVLA-style visual-controller
training path. The intended request interface grounds text, points, or masks
into an object and an optional contact region; the current implementation uses
explicitly supplied object identities and manual part-region annotations.
The initial scope is a five-fingered hand grasping among a few distractors.
Our hypothesis is that preserving teacher diversity and contact structure
improves requested-part grasping over matched pose-only or limited-diversity
supervision. This working draft defines the research question and evaluation
protocol; performance of the proposed part-conditioned student and real-robot
transfer remain to be established.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
Dexterous hands can use different finger combinations and contacts on the same
object. A policy should exploit this diversity when the user requests a part.
Selecting a watering can leaves a choice of feasible regions; requesting its
handle imposes a contact constraint. Choosing a preferred region also requires
semantics that a geometric grasp expert may not contain.

Dexonomy~\cite{dexonomy} provides existing taxonomy-based grasp expertise.
DexGraspVLA~\cite{dexgraspvla} provides a hierarchical reference for semantic
planning and visual action generation. We study whether synthetic execution can
transfer the former's expertise to a student in the latter family on the
FFW-SH5 / HX5-D20, without collecting new real grasp demonstrations.

Synthetic, target-oriented, closed-loop grasping and functional constraints
already have close precedents~\cite{clutterdexgrasp,demofungrasp}.
Changing the teacher alone does not establish novelty. The question is which
knowledge survives conversion into executable demonstrations, and whether its
preservation improves the learned student's requested-part behavior.

\textbf{Objectives, pending validation.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Develop a transfer mechanism associating teacher contacts with requested
regions while retaining useful grasp diversity during execution.
\item Measure contact/type fidelity from teacher to demonstration to student
under matched data and training budgets.
\item Evaluate held-out requested-part grasping, mild-clutter robustness, and
real-robot transfer without newly collected real grasp demonstrations.
\end{enumerate}
These are research objectives, not completed contributions.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\textbf{Grasp knowledge and execution.}
Dexonomy synthesizes taxonomy-conditioned grasps through annotated templates and
object-specific refinement~\cite{dexonomy}. UniDexGrasp already combines diverse
proposals with goal-conditioned execution and teacher--student
learning~\cite{unidexgrasp}. Pose-to-policy transfer itself is therefore not new.

\textbf{Synthetic supervision.}
DexGraspVLA's hierarchical planner and visual diffusion controller motivate the
student~\cite{dexgraspvla}. GraspVLA demonstrates synthetic action pretraining
with photorealistic rendering and domain randomization~\cite{graspvla}.
Neither precedent removes the need to test this hand's domain gaps.

ClutterDexGrasp learns a simulation RL teacher and distills it into a DP3 student
using partial point clouds for target-oriented closed-loop
grasping~\cite{clutterdexgrasp}. Here Dexonomy remains the source of grasp
knowledge; an optional RL executor learns to realize its goals with the arm.
This distinction motivates an experiment, not a claim of superiority.

\textbf{Part and functional constraints.}
DemoFunGrasp conditions on style and affordance and edits a single demonstration
through RL~\cite{demofungrasp}; it is not learning entirely from scratch.
DexVLG studies semantic dexterous grasp prediction~\cite{dexvlg}.
Part-conditioned pose prediction, executed grasping, and downstream functional
use require distinct evaluation. Our initial task is executed requested-part
grasping, not demonstrated pouring or tool use.

\section{TASK AND SUPERVISION}
Let $u$ denote the request, $I_t^h,I_t^w$ head and wrist images, and
$q_t\in\mathbb{R}^{27}$ the seven arm and twenty finger joint states.
The proposed grounding interface produces an object mask $M_t^o$, optional part
mask $M_t^p$, and an object-only versus part-constraint flag $b$:
\begin{equation}
(M_t^o,M_t^p,b)=G(u,I_t^h).
\end{equation}
Object text, object masks, and selection points identify the object. Explicit
part text, part masks, and contact points constrain the grasp region. A selection
point is not automatically an exact contact requirement.
The current synthetic-data interface accepts manual unions of axis-aligned boxes
in the object's local frame, in metres at its executed scale. The same region
definition labels visible pixels from depth and tests physical contact points.
Object identity, part mask, and part presence remain distinct inputs.
\todo{Implement automatic text/point grounding, define point modes and contact
neighborhoods, and evaluate mask tracking under occlusion.}

The proposed visual student predicts action chunks:
\begin{equation}
a_{t:t+H-1}\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid I_{\leq t}^h,I_{\leq t}^w,
q_{\leq t},M_{\leq t}^o,M_{\leq t}^p,b).
\end{equation}
It executes a prefix and observes again. Language enters through grounding;
the upstream visual controller does not directly consume raw text.
The implemented baseline uses one current observation, a 50 Hz control cadence,
and absolute position-servo targets ordered as seven right-arm then twenty finger
joints. It predicts $H=64$ targets and executes a configurable prefix, initially
eight, before observing again. Targets already contain any executor feedforward
offset; deployment does not add that offset a second time.
\todo{Validate horizon, prefix and observation-history choices in controlled runs.}

The setting permits existing human-annotated templates, pretrained foundation
models, and disclosed calibration. It excludes newly collected real-robot grasp
demonstrations for this platform, not all human information. Privileged state
can guide synthetic execution but must not enter the deployed visual student.

\section{PROPOSED TRANSFER METHOD}
\subsection{Teacher Candidates}
Represent a candidate conceptually as
\begin{equation}
g=(T_{oh},q^{\mathrm{pre}},q^{\mathrm{grasp}},q^{\mathrm{sq}},k,C),
\end{equation}
with object-relative hand transform, pregrasp/grasp/squeeze configurations,
source type $k$, and available contact annotations $C$. This is a proposed
normalized record, not a statement that every existing file has every field.

Current preparation retargets annotated Shadow templates to HX5-D20 and uses
Dexonomy refinement/evaluation. Anatomical correspondences are explicit;
arbitrary-hand automation is untested. Preserve geometry identity, scale, frames,
template revision, and original candidate identity.

\subsection{Region Association and Executable Demonstrations}
The region interface and execution gate associate manually requested regions with
teacher contacts. Each annotated contact-body group must carry positive normal
force inside the region; significant outside-region contacts on those annotated
bodies invalidate part compliance. This checks annotated groups, not an exact
taxonomy label or every possible contact on the hand. Geometric contacts are not
semantic part labels, and manual box regions are not automatic semantic grounding.
The current geometric feedback seeks missing object contacts; it does not by
itself plan an alternative region-compatible grasp when the original candidate
is unsuitable. Such executions remain failures under the part gate.
\todo{Validate region-compatible candidate selection and preservation objectives
across parts, contact-body groups and grasp types.}

Execute approach, closure, and sustained lift with the arm, table, and intended
distractors. IK/scripted execution is a baseline. An RL executor is an optional
means of realizing teacher goals, not a replacement for Dexonomy or a mandatory
paper contribution. Record original and adjusted candidates, failure reasons,
and resulting contact/type changes. Lifting with a different grasp is not
automatic evidence that the original knowledge was preserved.

The implemented non-RL collector screens complete dynamic home approaches along
candidate local directions, then applies contact/force feedback with bounded
multi-interval command previews. It preserves the complete simulator integration
state around IK and previews, and commits only accepted controller updates.
Lift progress pauses when contact or command acceptance is lost. Completion
requires a continuous measured contact-supported hold. This is a bounded local
executor, not a complete constrained motion planner or a hardware safety controller.

The current experimental RL executor uses privileged simulator state and staged
open/close goals. Its corrected reward uses discount-consistent potential
shaping, task completion, and disturbance/collision costs. Success requires
sustained lift with teacher contact-group compliance. This is an experimental
executor, not an established transfer method or a visual student.
\todo{Compare corrected non-RL and RL execution on fixed tasks and budgets;
demonstrate benefit before making the executor a claimed contribution.}

\subsection{Visual Student}
Record synchronized images, explicit conditions, states, and actions, retaining
full-scene RGB alongside object and part masks. Preserve teacher provenance.
The implemented schema stores pre-action head RGB/object mask, wrist RGB,
27-dimensional state/action, optional part mask, timing and conservative
per-physics-step contact/force/penetration evidence. Provenance includes original
teacher/geometry hashes, original and adapted targets, compiled simulator models
and complete initial states. Rejected trajectories remain separate from accepted
episodes and cannot enter a training split. Legacy image/action Zarr stores lack
the required quality evidence and are not automatically promoted to positives.

A workspace-owned architectural adaptation uses frozen DINOv2 head and wrist
encoders, separate object/part/presence mask channels, normalized joint state,
and an RDT-style action diffusion transformer. The reference configuration uses
twelve transformer layers, a fifty-step cosine noise schedule, epsilon prediction
and sixteen DDIM inference steps. Action/state normalization uses only training
episodes, and padded action targets are excluded from the objective. This is not
an upstream policy-checkpoint conversion or an implemented language planner.
Training, checkpoint inference, offline evaluation and saved-scene closed-loop
replay entry points exist. Only a small forward-only fixture has been checked;
local pretrained DINO assets and full-model forward validation remain required.
No training or weight update of this diffusion student has been performed.
\todo{Train after clean-data and model prerequisites pass; validate initialization,
visual augmentation, optimization and sampling against the reference design.}
First reproduce a small set of training tasks to test learning and interfaces;
this is not held-out generalization. Scripted demonstrations can train a student
that reobserves visually, but recovery depends on perturbation/correction coverage.

\section{IMPLEMENTATION AND EVIDENCE STATUS}
Hand preparation, Dexonomy synthesis, alternative scene search, a MuJoCo
recorder, a privileged PPO executor and the preparation components described
above exist. The current quality gate requires a clear home start, complete
approach, and a terminal contact-preserving hold above 10 cm for at least 0.5 s.
Default whole-trajectory limits include 2 mm robot--object penetration and 30 N
external normal-force sum per right digit; table/distractor collision and object
disturbance are separately checked. These are disclosed simulation-quality
settings, not hardware-certified bounds or final paper evaluation thresholds.
No clean accepted training set, trained diffusion policy, automatic grounding or
downstream knowledge-transfer benefit is established. A separate historical
small recurrent visual
behavior-cloning diagnostic connects one synthetic close/lift trajectory to
simulator policy execution. The open hand is initialized at the original teacher
grasp root pose, so this test excludes approach, clutter, and part conditioning.
It uses 350 pre-action frames at 50 Hz and 27-dimensional joint targets. The
scripted executor reproduces a sustained lift from this state, and the small
student also lifts in closed-loop replay. Neither preserves all five annotated
contact groups. This diagnostic validates a bounded data/learning/execution
connection, not the proposed contact-preserving transfer method, useful visual
feedback, or held-out generalization. The recurrent state may memorize timing;
blank-image inference is only an out-of-distribution sensitivity check.

A subsequent matched execution diagnostic retains the same teacher grasp but
screens six local approach directions. The default local $+Z$ route collides
with the object despite a clear final open-hand pose. A screened $+X$ route
reproduces the complete home--overhead--approach--close--lift sequence, including
two stored-command replays. This is one selected candidate, not an execution
success-rate estimate. All five contact groups exist under static squeeze-pose
placement, but dynamic execution in that fixed-control diagnostic never preserves all five. Increased squeeze,
arm gravity feedforward, and delayed thumb closure fail to restore compliance
in the tested settings. This separates a candidate-specific approach failure
from the unresolved contact-preserving closure problem. The visual student
has not yet been retrained or evaluated on this full approach trajectory.

A further privileged contact-feedback prototype regulates per-digit closure and
uses finger Jacobians with collision geometry to approach missing annotated
contact bodies. On the same selected task, three identical-state feedback runs
maintain all five contact groups with positive normal forces while lifted.
However, transient hand--object penetration reaches approximately 7.5 mm, so
these are diagnostic contact-lift positives rather than clean demonstrations.
Gentler settings fail to retain the lift, and a one-control-tick preview with
explicit 2 mm penetration and 30 N per-digit force limits stops before lifting.
These limits are diagnostic engineering choices, not hardware-certified bounds.
The result does not establish clean physical execution, semantic part fidelity,
held-out generalization, or a benefit to the intended visual student.

An independent replay audit identifies hidden simulator-state mutations in old
resets, IK diagnostics and post-step observation calls. Correcting observation
warmstart preservation barely changes the saved-command trajectory's penetration.
A tighter solver still gives approximately 7.49 mm maximum penetration, while
replaying the same 50 Hz actions at a 1 ms physics timestep loses five-group
compliance. These are open-loop numerical-sensitivity diagnostics, not reruns of
the feedback controller or evidence of numerical convergence. The audit also
records compiled object mass and the effect of object anti-spin friction on
finger contacts; neither nominal mass nor table-specific intent determines the
actual compiled contact model. The new collector retains these physical settings
and records their provenance rather than changing them to pass a quality gate.

A fresh schema-3 collection on the same selected candidate uses a two-control-
interval quality preview and records the full home path. Across 1124 intervals,
it reaches five contact groups and an interval-minimum rise of approximately
8.42 cm, but cannot complete the required contact-preserving hold above 10 cm.
It is therefore rejected for training. Peak penetration remains below 2 mm
and summed normal force below 30 N per digit in this run. Replaying its archived
full-precision actuator commands reproduces the recorded joint states and
physics-quality signals exactly. This validates execution and measurement
reproducibility on one task, not physical calibration or successful knowledge
transfer. No training was run during this preparation pass.

Earlier arm measurements had orientation/contact/sampling faults and are
withdrawn. A prior static-contact displacement diagnostic did not measure
physical lifting and cannot establish pose robustness. The stage ladder is a
hand-only replay/shake diagnostic; its arm rung is not implemented. Invalid
setups and unattempted rungs must be distinguished from measured failures.

Initial RL logs also mixed weighted/gated reward with contact metrics, omitted
timeout bootstrap, and did not seed policy initialization. They do not isolate
a cause of failure or prove RL ineffective. Their height-only success criterion
differs from the corrected contact-compliant sustained-lift criterion.
No numerical method-performance claim is made from these historical records.

\item Limited/type-unaware candidates versus diverse Dexonomy candidates.
\item Pose-only conversion versus the implemented contact/type-informed mechanism.
\item Object-only versus explicit-part input, changing only the requested part

\item Oracle grounding versus predicted grounding.

\section{LIMITATIONS}
One hand is implemented. Some part requests may be inaccessible or mechanically
ungraspable. Grounding errors may dominate; filtering may erase rare types.
Synthetic dynamics, friction, geometry, appearance, and timing can limit transfer.
Pretrained visual features do not eliminate these gaps.

The preparation implementation does not establish clean-data feasibility or
learned contact/part transfer. Student experiments are unfinished. If matched
comparisons show no benefit from preserved knowledge, revise the hypothesis.
Part grasping does not prove downstream function, and a bounded test set does
not justify universal object or embodiment claims.
